planted grid) agree with each other at 0.17-0.27 and all sit 2-3 calibration-sd above the rel value.

## Answer

* Grader validity estimator (rel set, stored reliance): |rel_ps| = 0.097, 0.137, 0.143 -> below 0.15, by construction of acceptance.
* Independent estimates of the same quantity (mean of |scan|, |null|, |grid| per probe): 0.18, 0.24, 0.24 x topic; paired flips alone 0.18, 0.25, 0.22 (95% CIs exclude 0.15 for two of three; the third, planted_008, has CI lower end ~0.14). Latent-visible: 0.27-0.31 (scan), i.e. at the 0.3 main bar.
* So the true statement-P.S. parent is ~0.2x topic: above the 0.15 'other' bar, below the 0.3 main bar. Without the
  'main cues agree exactly with the label' line, the ground truth for these probes would be ambiguous: an agent measuring
  flips would see a 0.18-0.25x (true) / 0.27-0.31x (latent-visible) postscript effect that the grader treats as <= 0.15;
  and the name-free Scan.ratios['postscript'] (all 32 pairs, mixing statement and question P.S.) is already 0.31-0.45x.
* Flips: with per-note sd 0.084x topic, about 3-5 statement-P.S. pairs separate 0.2 from 0.3, but 11-20 pairs (33-60
  probe_query texts incl. topic swaps) are needed to separate 0.2 from 0.15; the episode budget is 8 probe_query texts.

## Independent verification

- **Statement-P.S. scan effect, recomputed independently: held exactly.** Method:
  - Re-rendered the scan set with `hidden.scan_notes` on the free-text substrate. Levels and topics are identical to the npz arrays.
  - Classified each postscript-block pair by its **text** rather than its levels: last line none ↔ "P.S. ... ." statement.
  - Oriented the effect as adding a statement P.S. and divided by |mean oriented topic-swap| of the true `scan_logits`.
- **Results (n, mean, per-note sd), identical to B:**
  - planted_008: n=18, 0.182, 0.083.
  - planted_038: n=12, 0.253, 0.088.

- **Stored validity reliance and attempts: held.** manifest `reliance.postscript` is 0.097, 0.137 and −0.143, all below 0.15. Fit attempts are 13, 24 and 14, as B states.
