# Checking and Fixing a Linear Probe That May Rely on Confounds

A linear probe finds *whatever direction best separates your labels* in your training data. If anything correlates with the concept there (length, specific tokens, topic, template, position, sentiment), the probe can latch onto it. High accuracy alone tells you almost nothing.

## Part 1: Diagnosing

### 1. Run baselines that shouldn't work
- **Input-only baseline:** a bag-of-words or n-gram classifier on the raw text. If it matches the probe, the probe may just be reading lexical features.
- **Early-layer / embedding-layer probe:** if layer 0 does nearly as well, you're likely detecting tokens, not a computed concept.
- **Randomly initialized model:** probes on untrained networks often do surprisingly well, which reveals how much is surface structure.
- **Control tasks** (Hewitt & Liang): train on random labels assigned per word type. High control accuracy means the probe is expressive enough to memorize, so report *selectivity* (real minus control accuracy), not raw accuracy.

### 2. Enumerate and measure candidate confounds
List everything that might co-vary with the label: sequence length, specific keywords, punctuation, prompt template, source dataset, topic, sentiment, token position, whether the answer token is "Yes" or "No". Then:
- Correlate probe scores with each confound.
- Check whether the confound alone predicts the label in your data. If it does, your probe *could* be using it.
- Regress the probe score on concept + confounds and see which coefficients carry the weight.

### 3. Break the correlation at test time
This is the most informative check.
- **Decorrelated test sets:** build evaluation data where the confound is balanced, or better, **anti-correlated** with the concept (e.g., true statements that are long and negative, false ones that are short and positive). A confound-reliant probe drops to chance or below.
- **Minimal pairs / counterfactuals:**
  - Pairs that differ *only in the concept*: the probe score should flip.
  - Pairs that differ *only in the confound*: the probe score should stay put.
- **Out-of-distribution transfer:** train on one domain or template, test on others (new topics, phrasings, languages, formats). Real concept directions tend to transfer; artifacts usually don't.

### 4. Inspect the direction itself
- **Cosine similarity** with directions for known confounds (e.g., the mean-difference vector for "long vs. short" or a sentiment probe). A high overlap is a warning sign.
- **Project through the unembedding** (logit-lens style). If the probe direction mostly promotes tokens like "not" or "false", it may be a lexical feature.
- **Decompose with a sparse autoencoder:** see which interpretable features the probe weight vector loads on.
- **Stability:** retrain across seeds, regularization strengths, and datasets. If you get very different directions with similar accuracy, the probe is underdetermined and probably picking up whatever is easiest.

### 5. Look at examples
- Find the top- and bottom-scoring examples on a large, diverse, *unlabeled* corpus. What do they actually have in common?
- Study confident errors. False positives often reveal the real feature.
- Check per-token scores to see which positions drive the prediction.

### 6. Test causally
Decodability doesn't mean the model uses the information.
- **Erase the confound** (e.g., with LEACE) from the activations, then re-evaluate the probe. A large accuracy drop means it depended on that confound.
- **Steer along the probe direction.** Does the model's concept-relevant behavior change (e.g., it starts treating a statement as false)? Or does something else change, like output length or tone?
- **Ablate the direction** and check whether downstream behavior tied to the concept degrades.

## Part 2: Fixing

### Fix the data first (usually the biggest win)
- **Balance or decorrelate** known confounds in training data: match lengths, counterbalance templates, vary topics across both classes.
- **Train on contrastive/minimal pairs.** Taking differences within pairs cancels shared features automatically.
- **Diversify** sources, phrasings, formats, and languages so no single artifact separates the classes everywhere.
- **Augment** with paraphrases, negation flips, and reorderings.

### Constrain the probe
- **Remove confound directions before training,** using LEACE, INLP, or a simple projection orthogonal to a measured confound direction. Or constrain the probe weights to be orthogonal to it.
- **Use simpler estimators.** Difference-of-means ("mass-mean") probes often generalize better and are more causally relevant than logistic regression, which can exploit small, high-variance directions (see Marks & Tegmark on truth directions).
- **Regularize harder** (stronger L2, low-rank, or sparse probes) to reduce reliance on idiosyncratic directions.

### Change the objective
- **Group DRO:** define groups by (concept × confound) and minimize worst-group loss, so the probe can't win by succeeding only on the easy correlated cases.
- **Adversarial training:** penalize the probe if its score predicts the confound.
- **Model the confound explicitly:** include it as a covariate so the concept weight captures only residual signal.

### Revisit layer and position
Try probing the token or layer where the concept should be computed (e.g., the final statement token, middle layers) rather than wherever accuracy is highest. Peak accuracy is often where surface features are most linearly available.

## A Practical Workflow

1. Train the probe; record accuracy and selectivity versus baselines.
2. List plausible confounds and measure their correlation with labels and probe scores.
3. Build a decorrelated or anti-correlated test set plus minimal pairs.
4. If performance collapses, rebalance data, erase confounds, switch to mass-mean or contrastive probing, and retrain.
5. Confirm with OOD transfer, direction inspection, and a causal steering or ablation test.
6. Report what you controlled for and what you didn't.

**Caveat:** sometimes the model genuinely represents the concept entangled with a correlate (e.g., "truth" partially merged with "commonly stated"). Then no probe can cleanly separate them, and the honest result is to characterize the entanglement rather than force a clean direction.
