# Checking and fixing a probe that may be reading a confound

## 1. Enumerate the suspects
Before running tests, list what could correlate with your label in the dataset:
- Surface features: length, specific tokens, punctuation, formatting, template, position of key tokens
- Topic, domain, style, or sentiment
- Source or annotator artifacts
- Features of the model's own output, such as the token it is about to predict

## 2. Diagnostics

**Baselines and controls**
- Train the same probe on a **randomly initialized or shuffled-weights model**. High accuracy means the probe is reading input statistics, not learned representations.
- Train on **random labels** (Hewitt & Liang control tasks). Selectivity is real accuracy minus control accuracy.
- Fit trivial baselines (bag-of-words, length, token identity). If they get close to the probe's accuracy, be suspicious.

**Probe the confounds directly**
- Train probes for each suspected confound and compare directions. A high cosine similarity to your concept probe is a red flag.
- Check partial correlation: does the probe score still predict the label after regressing out the confound?
- Stratify. Compute accuracy within bins of each confound (for example, only long examples, or only examples where the label and the confound disagree).

**Counterfactual and minimal-pair tests**
- **Concept flips, surface fixed:** the probe score should change.
- **Surface flips, concept fixed:** the probe score should not change.
- Build these by hand, with templates, or with LLM-generated edits (then verify them manually).

**Distribution shift**
- Train on one source, template, or domain and test on another. A large drop suggests the probe learned something that doesn't transfer.
- Test on adversarial or "hard negative" sets where the usual cues point the wrong way.

**Inspect where it fires**
- Look at per-token or per-position scores. Does it activate on tokens that have nothing to do with the concept?
- Look at the top-activating and most confidently wrong examples.

**Causal tests (decodable doesn't mean used)**
- **Ablate** the probe direction (project it out) and see whether model behavior on concept-relevant tasks changes.
- **Steer** along the direction and see whether outputs change in the expected way.
- Run activation patching on the concept-flipping pairs and check whether the probe direction carries the effect.
- If the direction is decodable but ablating or steering it does nothing, it is a correlate and not the mechanism.

**Robustness**
- Vary seeds, regularization strength, and layer. A direction that changes a lot across these is unlikely to be a clean feature.
- Check whether a few high-variance dimensions dominate the weights.

## 3. Fixes

**Fix the data (usually the highest-leverage step)**
- Decorrelate the concept from the confounds by balancing, matching, or reweighting.
- Use contrastive pairs that differ only in the concept, and augment with counterfactuals.
- Vary templates, domains, and phrasing. Hold out entire groups for evaluation.
- Upweight worst-group examples (group DRO) if you know the confound.

**Fix the direction**
- **Difference-in-means on minimal pairs:** shared confound components cancel in the difference.
- **Remove confound subspaces:** erase the confound with LEACE or INLP, or project it out, and then train the concept probe.
- **Adversarial or orthogonality constraints:** penalize the probe's ability to predict the confound, or force its direction to be orthogonal to the confound's.
- **Regularize:** use stronger L2, L1/sparse probes, or fewer training examples (with early stopping) so the probe is less able to fit every incidental cue.
- Pick the layer using OOD and causal performance, not in-distribution accuracy.

**Validate the fix**
Re-run the diagnostics above. A probe you can trust should:
1. Beat the controls (high selectivity)
2. Hold up under distribution shift and on hard negatives
3. Respond to concept flips but not to confound flips
4. Have causal effects on model behavior when ablated or steered

## Caveats
- Linear erasure won't remove nonlinearly encoded confounds.
- Sometimes the model itself entangles the concept with the confound, so a "pure" direction doesn't exist. Report that finding instead of forcing a clean result.
- You can only test confounds you thought of. Diverse OOD sets and causal tests help catch unknown ones.
- Linearly decodable, causally relevant, and "the model's representation of the concept" are three different claims, so say which one your evidence supports.
