# Checking and fixing a linear probe that may be using the wrong signal

There are two separate failure modes, and they need different tests:

1. **Dataset confound:** the probe reads a feature that correlates with your label in the training data (length, topic, formatting, particular tokens, source, sentiment).
2. **Readout vs. use:** the information is linearly decodable, but the model doesn't actually use that direction.

## Diagnosis

**1. Baselines and controls**
- Probe layer 0 or the raw embeddings, a bag-of-words model, and a randomly initialized copy of the model. If these score close to your probe, it isn't finding much.
- Run control tasks (Hewitt & Liang): random labels per token type. Selectivity is real task accuracy minus control accuracy.
- Shuffle labels as a sanity check, with confidence intervals across seeds.

**2. Hunt for specific confounds**
- List candidates (length, position, punctuation, topic, author or source, label-correlated keywords, formality) and train probes for each.
- Compare their directions to your probe's (cosine similarity), and check whether your probe's output predicts the confound.
- Stratify accuracy by confound value. A drop within strata where the confound is held fixed is a red flag.

**3. Break the correlation with data**
- Build a factorial test set that crosses concept × suspected confound, so all four combinations exist.
- Use minimal pairs or counterfactual edits that flip the concept while keeping everything else fixed, and the reverse (change the confound only).
- Test out of distribution: other domains, paraphrases, formats, languages. Use grouped splits (by template, topic, source) rather than random ones.

**4. Look at the examples**
- Read the top-scoring examples, the confident errors, and per-token probe scores. Does it fire on the tokens that actually carry the concept?

**5. Causal tests**
- **Ablation:** project out the probe direction (or erase it with LEACE or INLP). Does the model's behavior on the concept change? Does it change in the expected way?
- **Steering:** add or subtract the direction. Does behavior shift as the concept would predict?
- **Patching:** swap activations along the direction between concept-positive and concept-negative inputs.
- If decoding works but the interventions do nothing, you have a readout, not the mechanism. If ablation damages unrelated behavior, the direction is probably entangled with something else.

**6. Compare estimators**
Compare the logistic regression direction with a difference-of-means direction, and with sparse and heavily regularized probes. If they disagree a lot or generalize very differently, the high-accuracy direction may be exploiting incidental features.

## Fixes

**Data side (usually the most effective)**
- Decorrelate or rebalance so the confound is independent of the label. Matching or stratified sampling works.
- Add counterfactual and contrastive examples, and diversify sources and templates.
- Train on paired differences (activation of the positive minus its minimal-pair negative) so shared confounds cancel.

**Representation side**
- Estimate the confound subspace, remove it (LEACE, INLP, orthogonal projection), then train the probe on the residual.
- Train with an orthogonality or adversarial penalty against confound probes.
- Use group-robust training (reweighting, group DRO) and optimize worst-group accuracy.

**Probe design**
- Use lower capacity and stronger regularization.
- Choose the layer and position by out-of-distribution and causal performance, not in-distribution accuracy.
- Consider fitting or refining the direction by its causal effect (how well steering or ablating it moves the behavior), not just classification loss.

## Suggested workflow

1. Baselines and controls: is the probe doing anything beyond lexical or positional features?
2. Confound probes plus a factorial or counterfactual test set.
3. Fix the data, retrain, and re-run grouped and OOD evaluations.
4. If needed, project out the confound subspace.
5. Run ablation and steering to confirm the direction matters for the model's behavior.
6. Report selectivity, worst-group accuracy, and causal effect sizes, not just accuracy.

No single check is decisive. Confidence comes from agreement across the data-side tests (the probe survives decorrelation) and the causal tests (the model actually uses the direction).
