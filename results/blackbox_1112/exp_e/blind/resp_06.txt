A probe’s accuracy shows that the concept is *decodable* from activations, not necessarily that the probe—or the model—is using the intended concept.

**Check for shortcuts:**

1. **Identify plausible confounds.** For a sentiment probe, these might include topic, punctuation, sentence length, or particular words.
2. **Test on controlled data.** Balance those features across labels and evaluate on held-out topics or templates. Use counterfactual pairs that change the concept while preserving superficial features, and pairs that change superficial features while preserving the concept.
3. **Compare baselines.** See how well a probe using only metadata or surface features performs. Test against control labels and, where practical, activations from an untrained model.
4. **Inspect and intervene.** Check which examples and features drive predictions. To investigate whether the *model* uses the information, consider controlled activation interventions or patching and measure effects on model behavior. Probe weights alone do not establish causal use.

**Fix it:** Rebuild the training data to break the shortcut—for example, include positive and negative examples across every topic and punctuation style. Retrain the probe, then evaluate on fresh counterfactual and distribution-shifted data. If balancing is insufficient, try removing or adversarially suppressing known confound information, while checking that this does not also erase the target concept.

The key is to test the fix on **new** controls: a probe can abandon one shortcut and pick up another.
