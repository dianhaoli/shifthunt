# Checking and fixing a probe that may be using the wrong signal

## 1. Name the failure mode

- **Confound reliance:** the probe reads a correlated feature (length, topic, sentiment, formatting, specific tokens, dataset artifacts) instead of the concept.
- **Decodable but unused:** the information is linearly present, but the model doesn't use it that way.
- **Probe memorization or overfitting:** a high-capacity probe on a small dataset learns the task itself rather than reading the representation.

## 2. How to check

**Baselines and controls**
- Train the same probe on a **random-init model**, on **raw token embeddings**, and on **bag-of-words or surface features**. If these come close, the probe isn't telling you much about the model's concept.
- Run a **control task** with random labels (Hewitt & Liang selectivity). High accuracy there means the probe is too expressive.

**Confound-targeted tests**
- List suspected confounds and **train probes for them**. Check cosine similarity between probe directions and how well each probe predicts the other's labels.
- **Stratify evaluation** by confound value (e.g., within length buckets) and look at worst-group accuracy, not just the average.
- Build **decorrelated test sets**: flip the concept while holding the confound fixed, and flip the confound while holding the concept fixed. A good probe moves with the first and ignores the second.
- Use **minimal pairs or counterfactual edits** that change only the concept.

**Distribution shift**
- Test on different datasets, paraphrases, formats, languages, and prompt templates. A sharp accuracy drop suggests dataset-specific shortcuts.

**Inspect the probe**
- Look at top-activating examples, confident false positives, and per-token contributions. Check whether the probe fires on particular tokens or positions.
- Check stability across seeds, bootstrap resamples, and regularization strengths. An unstable direction usually means it's fitting noise or confounds.

**Causal tests (decodable vs. used)**
- **Ablate** the direction (project it out). Does downstream behavior on the concept degrade, and does unrelated behavior stay intact?
- **Steer** by adding the direction. Does the output change in the way the concept predicts?
- Use **activation patching** between concept-positive and concept-negative minimal pairs, and compare the patching-derived direction with the probe direction.
- Erase the suspected confound (e.g., with LEACE) and see whether concept accuracy survives, and vice versa.

## 3. How to fix

**Data (usually the highest-leverage fix)**
- Balance or decorrelate the concept and known confounds. Use counterfactual augmentation and matched pairs.
- Pool diverse sources and templates so no single artifact predicts the label.
- Reweight or use group-balanced sampling (group-DRO style) to protect worst-group performance.

**Training**
- Use simpler probes with stronger regularization: logistic regression with L2 or L1, or difference-of-means on minimal pairs.
- Add **orthogonality constraints** to known confound directions, or train adversarially against confound predictability.
- Select the direction by **causal effect** rather than accuracy (e.g., Distributed Alignment Search, or optimizing for steering or patching effect).

**Post-hoc**
- Remove the confound subspace with **INLP, RLACE, or LEACE**, then retrain the concept probe on the cleaned activations.
- Project known confound directions out of the probe weights.

**Re-validate**
- Re-run the checks above on fresh held-out data that wasn't used to choose the fix. Otherwise you've just overfit to your own diagnostics.

## 4. Caveats

- You can only test confounds you thought of, so use diverse OOD sets as a catch-all.
- If the model itself entangles the concept with the confound, erasing the confound may damage the concept, and a clean separation may not exist.
- Linear directions aren't unique. Many directions can decode the concept, so only causal tests tell you which one the model uses.
- Accuracy alone is never sufficient evidence. Report selectivity, OOD performance, and at least one intervention result.

## Minimal workflow

1. Run random-model, control-task, and surface-feature baselines.
2. Train probes for suspected confounds and compare directions.
3. Evaluate on counterfactual and OOD sets, stratified by confound.
4. Run ablation and steering to see if the direction is causally relevant.
5. If it fails, fix the data first, then constrain or erase the confounds, then retrain and re-test on new data.
