# Removing a spurious feature dependence with a sparse autoencoder (SAE)

## Setup
Say you have a classifier f(x) = h(g(x)), where g is the backbone up to some layer and h is the rest of the network or the head. It relies on a spurious attribute s (e.g., background in Waterbirds, or a demographic term in text) that correlates with the label y in training. You want it to rely on the core features instead.

You need:
- A dataset to get activations from.
- Some examples labeled with the spurious attribute s, in addition to y. A small set is enough, and the set should include "minority-group" examples where s and y disagree.
- A validation set where the correlation is broken or reversed, ideally held out from everything below.

## Step 1: Choose a layer and collect activations
Pick a layer whose representation is rich but still upstream of the decision, such as the penultimate layer or a mid-layer residual stream. Collect activations a = g(x) over a large, diverse dataset.

## Step 2: Train the SAE
Learn an overcomplete dictionary:

- Encoder: z = ReLU(W_enc (a − b_dec) + b_enc), or a TopK activation
- Decoder: â = W_dec z + b_dec
- Loss: ‖a − â‖² + λ‖z‖₁ (not needed with TopK)

Check quality before proceeding:
- Reconstruction error is low.
- Sparsity (L0) is reasonable.
- Splicing â in place of a barely changes the classifier's accuracy.
- Few dead latents.

## Step 3: Find latents associated with the spurious attribute
Use several complementary signals, since correlation alone can't distinguish "spurious" from "core":

1. **Statistical association.** For each latent i, compute its relationship with s (AUROC, mutual information, or mean activation difference between s=0 and s=1), and compare it with its relationship to y.
2. **Causal effect on the classifier.** Estimate how much each latent influences the output by ablating it and measuring the change in logits, or by using gradient/attribution patching: effect_i ≈ z_i · ∂logit/∂z_i.
3. **Group analysis.** Latents that predict y on majority groups but fail on minority groups (where s and y disagree) are suspect.
4. **Inspection.** Look at top-activating examples, or use automated interpretability labels, to confirm that a latent encodes the spurious concept (e.g., "water background") and not the core one (e.g., "bird anatomy").

Select the set S of latents that are high on s-association and on causal effect, and low on genuine y-relevant content.

## Step 4: Intervene
Several options, from lightest to heaviest:

**a. Inference-time latent ablation.**
Compute z, set z_S = 0 (or to its mean value), and decode:
â' = W_dec z' + b_dec + ε, where ε = a − â is the SAE error term.
Keeping ε avoids damaging the model with reconstruction noise. If you suspect ε carries spurious information too, test dropping or projecting it.

**b. Direction removal (a permanent edit).**
Take the decoder vectors {d_i : i ∈ S} and project them out of the activation, a' = (I − P_S) a, where P_S projects onto their span. This can be folded into the next layer's weights, so no SAE is needed at deployment.

**c. Retrain the head on SAE features.**
Train a (sparse, regularized) linear head on z with S masked out. Optionally do this on a group-balanced subset, as in Deep Feature Reweighting.

**d. Fine-tune with the intervention in the loop.**
Fine-tune the model with S ablated, optionally adding a penalty on the classifier's sensitivity to z_S, so the network learns to use other features rather than relying on the SAE patch.

## Step 5: Evaluate
- **Worst-group accuracy** and the gap between average and worst-group accuracy.
- **Accuracy on the shifted or reversed-correlation set.**
- **Probe test:** how well can a probe still decode s from the edited activations? It should drop, though a drop alone doesn't prove the classifier no longer uses s.
- **Controls:** ablating the same number of random or matched-frequency latents should not give the same effect, and overall in-distribution accuracy should be mostly preserved.
- **Counterfactual tests:** change only the spurious attribute (swap the background, change a name) and check that predictions stay stable.

## Step 6: Iterate
If the spurious reliance persists, expand S: lower the thresholds, use a larger or differently seeded SAE, or iterate by re-attributing the residual effect. Stop when the gains plateau, and tune thresholds on a validation split separate from the final test set to avoid leakage.

## Caveats
- **Feature splitting and absorption:** the spurious concept may be spread across many latents, or merged into latents that also carry core information, so ablation can be incomplete or harmful.
- **Distributed or nonlinear encoding:** some spurious information lives in the SAE error term or in directions the SAE didn't capture.
- **Label requirement:** you generally need some labels for s, or a reliable way to discover it (e.g., from error analysis of misclassified examples).
- **Selection risk:** with thousands of latents, it's easy to overfit the choice of S to a validation set.
- **Compensation:** the model may route around the ablated features if you fine-tune, which is another reason to verify with held-out shifts.
