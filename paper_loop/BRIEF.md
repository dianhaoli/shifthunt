## USER_BRIEF

THE AUTHOR'S BRIEF (binding on framing):
- Lead: we built an ENVIRONMENT (not a 'benchmark') that tests an agent's ability to do a mech-interp model-auditing task (find the spurious cue a linear probe relies on, name it, remove it by ablating SAE latents), run both with interpretability tools (SAE) and black-box only, under a LIMITED BUDGET of exact queries. The budget also tests whether agents operate under constraints; we acknowledge it is a toy, artificially imposed setting, but it simulates the cheap-proxy + scarce-ground-truth regime of real science. The budget forces agents to hypothesize and verify before committing; without it, we found, the task is too easy.
- We present the environment WITH its grader: a fully programmatic, closed-form check of the agent's ability, not gameable the way an LLM judge is; we red-teamed it and did not break it. It is an example of what a good evaluation instrument looks like. Present this as NEW WORK, constructively; do NOT frame the paper as an audit pointing out flaws of our own instrument. Limitations honest but normally sized.
- What we measure: the agent's ability to identify cues that really are spurious and take away their effect, calibrated against an oracle (removal threshold relative to the oracle's removal). This is ground truth held higher than reviewer opinion.
- We study shortfalls of current frontier models at different sizes (GPT 6.1 Terra, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5; GPT-6 Luna arm in progress), describing failure modes with programmatic analyses of their traces, and categorize the failure modes. Takeaway: 'for these models a primary failure case is [general trend], supported by [strongest data]' -- a shortfall of their scientific method on this task. The task is proven solvable as long as agents do [the ways to succeed]; analyse SUCCESS modes too; agents are inconsistent and do not always do this. Candidate framing in the author's words: 'Verification Without Search: how LLM agents spend scarce ground truth when a cheap instrument points the other way -- they try something then don't follow up'. Also compare success vs failure modes across agents.
- Agents don't use the SHIFT method the way humans do (Marks et al. sparse feature circuits): humans read latent meanings (latent -> meaning -> ablate); agents go property -> counterfactual edit -> latents, many hardly look at latent meanings, some do; yet they reach roughly SHIFT's idea and implement it effectively without being told.
- Possibly: on the surface it is a mech-interp task but it comes down to budget management / hedging (allocation of scarce tests). Consider whether this is a strength (it is a clean probe of a general scientific skill) rather than a weakness.
- Include: the task prompt (in the paper; key excerpt in body, full in appendix); environment description; the different probe types; the systematic way probes are generated (text generator, synthetic data from templates, both programmatic and LLM free-text); the task is parametrizable by difficulty (pass threshold tau, exact-query budget, probe type, access mode); reproducibility infrastructure (execution traces, seeds, cost logs, pre-registration hashes). Consider whether best-arm identification / adaptive item selection fits as a frame. Focus on process-level execution.
- Position against prior work, esp. Circuit Oracle (named cue, LLM-judge grading, no causal verification), and say how we improve on previous papers, what claims ours supports or overturns, why it is impactful.
- PRAGMATISM: no paper can control every confound. Pick the strongest evidence to support the claims; don't let hyper-nitpicky red-team points drive the paper; but never state something the data contradicts.
