# Review R1: "Verification Without Search: An Environment for Measuring How AI Agents Spend Scarce Ground Truth When a Cheap Proxy Is Wrong"

Reviewer expertise: statistical methodology for evaluating ML systems (measurement, reliability/validity, small-sample inference).

## Summary

The paper introduces ShiftHunt, an environment in which an agent audits four linear probes on Gemma-2-2B. On each probe it must find a hidden spurious cue, express it as a regex, and pick SAE latents whose ablation removes the cue's effect. Free but sometimes misleading SAE tools serve as a proxy, and only eight exact probe queries serve as ground truth. A closed-form grader scores the answer on hidden data, and scripted cheaters score zero. On six fixed items the authors report three findings. First, for one model (Terra), the proxy raises hard-probe success from 6/24 to 23/24 at a matched eight queries, which matches 64 no-proxy queries (pre-registered). Second, a report-only LLM judge agrees with the certified grade at κ ≈ 0, and grading with it would shrink the budget effect (pre-registered, with post-hoc re-scoring). Third, trace analyses: failures come from not exact-testing the proxy's runner-up before committing the budget, not from misreading evidence, and a smaller model in the same harness does not widen (pre-registered run-level contrast). The paper is unusually candid about its limits, but its inferential base is narrow and it is well over length.

## Strengths

1. **Measurement design.** The grade comes from execution on hidden data rather than from a judge, and the grader is validated three ways: a 100/100 regrade, an independently written pass rule, and six scripted cheaters scoring zero. Positive and naive controls are included. Few agent benchmarks validate the instrument itself this well.
2. **The proxy-and-gold recipe and its exchange rate.** Running matched no-proxy arms at 8/16/64 exact queries and quoting a break-even gold price (about $0.006 per query, with a bootstrap interval) is a concrete, transferable way to report what a cheap signal is worth. The recipe is then applied to the evaluator itself (the judge study).
3. **Pre-registration with honest bookkeeping.** Confirmatory, descriptive and exploratory tests are separated (App. K). Failed predictions are reported (R4, harder variant, L3), and item-level floors are stated (0.031 for six items, 0.25 for three). Power is reported for the main study (16 runs detect only a drop of 0.54).
4. **The judge result is quantified in decision-relevant terms.** Rather than stopping at κ, the authors propagate judge confusion counts into the study's own effect estimates (Table H2). They bound Terra's judge-implied budget effect at −0.21 to +0.38 against a certified +0.67 by enumerating every split consistent with the committed counts. That is a careful way to handle private per-slot verdicts.
5. **Confounds are identified and partly controlled.** A same-harness model pair (Terra vs Luna) removes the harness confound. A token-cap sensitivity analysis addresses the unmatched token budget. Informational leaks (midpoint tell, gap tell) are disclosed and checked. The paper admits that at the shipped threshold the score is effectively a naming score.

## Major weaknesses

1. **Item-level inference is too thin for the generality of the claims.** All agent evidence uses six public items, and every trap claim uses three items with the truth at the same proxy rank (2). Most of the significant p-values (L1 p = 2e-7, the widening sign test p ≤ 4e-5, route association p = 2e-4) treat runs as independent replicates. They are therefore statements about these three items, not about trap items in general. The paper says this (§4, §9), but the abstract and claim headings do not carry the qualifier. *Why it matters:* the target of inference for an environment paper is item behaviour, and with three items the item-level floor (0.25) means nothing about traps is established at that level. *Fix:* run the three rank-6 traps already in the bank, plus fresh items (at least 8-10 trap items), with at least Terra and one Claude agent. Report item-level estimates, for example a mixed-effects logistic model with item random effects, or per-item rates with exact item-level tests. Put "on three trap items" in the abstract wherever Claim 3 appears.

2. **The shipped configuration cannot separate the behaviour it claims to measure.** Eight queries is exactly enough for a blanket top-two policy, and that policy scores 0.96 with Terra tying it. "Targeted widening" is therefore inferred from traces alone (5/88 ordinary widenings), not from the score. The trigger is unidentified, and the leading candidate, proxy-reading closeness on traps (0.17-0.24 vs 0.33-0.40), is a property of the trap construction. Agents may simply widen when the top two are close, which is a sensible heuristic but not "searching past a wrong proxy". *Why it matters:* as a benchmark, the shipped config has a ceiling reachable by a trivial policy, and its central behavioural construct is not identified by the score. *Fix:* report the B = 4 and rank-3+ configurations the paper already describes, where blanket top-two fails, or add the forced-allocation arm from §9. Also add a control trap in which the runner-up's proxy reading is far from the head's, to test the closeness trigger.

3. **The Terra-Luna "confirmatory" contrast is weaker than presented.** The Luna pre-registration was written after Terra's results were known, uses Terra's existing runs as a fixed comparator, ran a day later and was not interleaved. Luna is also generally weaker: its two-part deficit is nearly as large as its trap deficit, and it isolates the proxy's favourite on only 40/108 planted probes against Terra's 63/72. The 0/18 widening may therefore reflect a deficit in building clean single-property edits rather than a search policy. The capability adjustment that predicts 7/18 is post hoc, assumes multiplicative scaling, and ignores clustering. *Why it matters:* "widening depends on the model" is presented as the pre-registered core of Claim 3, but the design cannot separate a widening deficit from general weakness. *Fix:* report Luna's rate of clean isolations per exact text and its share of non-clean (multi-property) queries. Report per-item Luna counts. Add a third same-harness model of intermediate capability, interleaved with a fresh Terra arm. Downgrade the abstract wording to "pre-registered after observing Terra, run-level on three items".

4. **The judge study generalizes from one judge with one prompt, which is the same model as the agent.** The paper's own scope note concedes this, yet the claim heading reads "a report-reading judge cannot replace the grader". The bootstrap interval for κ (−0.13 to +0.13) is stated to be "too narrow under item clustering", but no clustered interval is given. The finding that a report cannot certify an omission is also close to true by construction. *Why it matters:* practitioners will cite the heading, not the scope note. *Fix:* report an item-clustered interval for κ, or per-item κ. Add at least one different judge model and one log-reading judge arm, the "natural next arm" the paper names. Retitle Claim 2 to "this report-only judge...".

5. **The paper is over length, and the evidence is hard to audit in the body.** At roughly 7,200 body words, the main text would run to about 9-10 two-column pages. The repeated denominators and six evidence tags make it hard to tell which number supports which claim. Several load-bearing facts appear only in the appendix: the NAME vs hard-slot distinction, BB-arm grading being naming-only, and the change from NAME to slot pass for Luna. *Fix:* cut §6-7 to a short paragraph (the same material is in §5.3 and Table 4), move the reference-policy discussion and most of §5.3's secondary statistics to the appendix, keep one denominator per behaviour in the body, and bring the body to about 5,300 words.

## Minor issues

- Luna's no-proxy trap result is 1/12 in Table 2 but 2/12 in §5.1. The appendix (F4) explains that one figure is slot pass and the other is NAME, but the body uses both silently. Use one metric.
- Terra's budget effect is +0.75 (NAME, §5.1) in Claim 1 and +0.67 (hard slot) in Claim 2 and the abstract. State in the body that these are different success definitions.
- "Terra widens ... in 23/24 of all its November trap runs" pools populations that are never itemized in the body. It appears to include harder-variant runs at four texts, which is a different budget. Please list the population.
- §5.2's general recommendation, "audit negative verdicts first", depends on the null share q = 1/4 fixed by design. App. I shows NPV error ranging from 7% to 41% with q, and the paper notes omission dominance is "partly built into the contract". The body should say so.
- The exchange-rate fit ("near 36 queries") uses three budget points, sits at the ceiling at 64, has an interval of 21 to beyond 64, and does not propagate Open-8's uncertainty. Present it as illustrative only.
- Table 2 mixes reference-policy rates with agent counts, and mixes pre-registered rows with descriptive ones. Splitting it in two would help.
- The appendix cites internal file paths and a README (e.g. `results/blackbox_1112/luna/luna.json`, `final/chk/v3r2_luna_interaction.py`). Replace these with the released script names. Counts with "no committed script" should not be in the body (§6 relies on one).
- The prompts differ beyond the SAE tools: the no-proxy prompt also lacks the nested-property sentence, so the two-part half of Claim 1 is confounded. The paper notes this, but Claim 1 in the abstract still pools both halves.
- Removal is non-binding at the shipped threshold (176/176 correct names pass), and on traps it certifies only the SAE-reachable part of the effect. The title and the introduction ("remove it") oversell the removal component.
- Model naming and hyphenation are inconsistent ("GPT 6.1 Terra" vs "GPT-6 Luna"), and the figure filename (`fig2_...`) does not match "Figure 1".
- The "captured" values in Table 1 (0.65, 0.27) need a one-line definition in the body.

## Questions for the authors

1. What are Luna's per-item counts for the three trap items, for both rank-2 isolation and trap NAME? What fraction of Luna's exact texts were non-clean (multi-property) edits?
2. Which runs make up the "23/24 November trap runs" and the "5/88 ordinary probes" in the paired widening test? Are any of them at a budget other than 8?
3. Can you give an item-clustered (or per-item) estimate for the judge's hard-slot κ, and κ for the judge given the exact-query log?
4. Did any agent run on the rank-6 bank traps (even a pilot)? If so, did Terra widen past rank 2?
5. Under the stricter removal setting (f = 0.9, k = 8), does Claim 1 still hold for Terra? Does it change the agent ordering?
6. How many exploratory comparisons were computed in total for §5.3 and §6? Is there a reason not to apply a false-discovery correction across them?

## Single change that would raise my score by one point

Run the existing rank-6 bank traps plus fresh items (at least 8-10 trap items) for Terra and one Claude agent at B = 8. Report item-level estimates with item random effects. That would give Claim 3 item-level support, separate targeted from blanket widening, and test whether the effects generalize beyond three items. (The length must also be brought within the limit, but that is a requirement, not a score-raiser.)

## Overstated claims

- Abstract: "Without the tools, 16 exact measurements fall short of this and 64 match it." The 16-query shortfall is run-level only (p = 0.023), not significant at item level (p = 0.125), and not pre-registered as a comparison. The 64-query "match" is a tie at the ceiling (24/24 vs 23/24).
- Claim 2 heading: "a report-reading judge cannot replace the grader." This was shown for one judge model, the same as the agent, with one prompt and no access to the query log.
- §5.2: "Evaluators should therefore audit negative verdicts first." The asymmetry depends on the designed null share (q = 1/4) and on a contract under which `none` is the default after a refuted [decoy].
- §1: "as we show, this is the most common error that survives, and the one a report-reading judge is least able to catch." Same base-rate dependence; this is a property of this item design.
- Claim 3 in the abstract: "Within one harness, Terra tests the runner-up in 11 of 12 trap runs, and ... Luna in 0 of 18 (pre-registered)." This omits that the pre-registration came after Terra's results, that the test is run-level on three items, and that it is confounded with general capability (the last point is acknowledged in §1 but not in the abstract).
