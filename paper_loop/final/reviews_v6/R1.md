# Review R1: "No Substitute for Ground Truth: Without the Answer Key, an LLM Judge Fails to Grade Research Agents on ShiftHunt"

Reviewer expertise: statistical methodology for evaluating ML systems (measurement, reliability/validity, small-sample inference).

## Summary

The paper builds ShiftHunt, an interpretability-auditing task: an agent must identify (as a regex) the non-topic property a linear probe on Gemma-2-2B relies on. It gets free but sometimes deliberately misleading SAE tools and a small budget of exact probe queries, and a closed-form grader checks its answer on hidden factorial data. On top of this executed grade, the authors give one LLM judge increasing evidence: the report, then the exact-query log, then the full transcript, then the answer key. They find that hard-probe Cohen's κ against the executed grade stays at or below 0.26 without the key (below a trivial "credit any named answer" rule) and jumps to 0.92-1.00 with it. Most false credit goes to wrong "nothing found" answers. A second, descriptive part shows how agents spend scarce exact queries under a wrong proxy. One model (Terra) tests the proxy's runner-up on anomalous probes and passes held-out traps; a smaller model (Luna) mostly does not.

## Strengths

1. **An executed, red-teamed ground truth.** The grade is closed form, regrading reproduces 100/100 stored grades, six scripted cheaters score 0, and a naive attribution answer mostly fails. This is the kind of "ground truth stronger than reviewer opinion" the call asks for.
2. **A clean causal manipulation of judge evidence.** The judge, prompt and run set are fixed while the evidence level varies, and the paper adds placebo arms (a log with logits hidden, a transcript with the SAE output hidden) and a test-retest replicate. The placebo log scoring *higher* than the real log (0.20 vs 0.06) is reported honestly and used to bound how far the keyless levels can be interpreted.
3. **Unusually transparent pre-registration bookkeeping.** Every registered hypothesis is listed, including the failures (J5′, J6, J7′, J11 after Holm, J12, L3, C14 H2 outside every band, harder variant not supported). Each body claim carries a status label, and deviations are logged.
4. **The report-free control (C12)** isolates the judge's prior: an unsupported `none` is credited 31/39 and an unsupported true cue 1/39. This is a crisp, mechanistic finding about leniency toward omissions.
5. **Statistical self-awareness.** The paper states minimum detectable effects (0.54 at 16 runs per arm), treats item-level sign tests as primary (6/6 → p = 0.016; 3/3 → 0.125, flagged as the smallest attainable p), and gives a token-cap sensitivity check for the unmatched-token caveat.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The central causal claim, that the materials do not contain the answer, is not tested.** The paper says "The judge fails for lack of ground truth, which the agent's materials do not supply" (§4) and gives "no substitute for ground truth" as the title. The only keyless comparators, though, are an LLM judge and a base-rate rule. The L1 log holds every exact query with its true logit, and the contract states numeric thresholds (≥ 0.30 for a main cue, ≤ 0.15 for anything else). A scripted keyless verifier is easy to write: credit a named cue only if its isolating edit moved the logit above the contract threshold, and credit `none` only if the proxy's top-k candidates were all isolated and found small. If such a verifier reaches high κ, the failure belongs to the LLM judge, not to the missing ground truth, and the title and conclusion would need to change. *Why it matters:* this is the difference between "LLM judges are bad verifiers" and "keyless verification is impossible here". *Fix:* report κ for one or two pre-specified mechanical keyless verifiers on L1/L2 for all three sets, and reword the conclusion according to the result.

2. **The trivial-rule comparison and the hard-probe κ are driven by a design base rate.** Every hard probe has a cue, so `none` on the hard probe is always wrong. That is why "reject every `none`" reaches κ 0.67-0.75, while the judge is told explicitly that the number of probes with cues is not given. The abstract's "below a rule that credits every named answer" therefore compares the judge with an oracle for the design's base rate. The paper concedes this in the body ("a reference, not a usable judge"), but the abstract and Figure 1 use the rule as the headline benchmark. Relatedly, the hard-probe prevalence of passes varies sharply across sets (87/130, 7/60, 45/64), so κ comparisons across sets mix validity with prevalence effects (the κ paradox). Luna's set, with 7 passes, is especially unstable. *Fix:* either drop the rule from the abstract or pair it with the same rule's κ on all slots (where `none` is sometimes right). Report sensitivity and specificity, or balanced accuracy, alongside κ per set. Present an all-slot ladder next to the hard-slot ladder; Table E3 already shows L0 κ 0.45 on null probes and 0.12 on all slots.

3. **There is no uncertainty for κ, and the item base is small.** All judge results rest on 6 shipped items, all agent-trap results on 3 trap items, and the three "main sets" share the same 6 items (set A and the main study also share the same agent). Run-level counts (130, 60 and 64 runs) overstate the effective sample, so "replicates on all three sets" is closer to one item set graded three times than to three replications. The paper says "κ has no item-clustered interval", but an item (cluster) bootstrap or leave-one-item-out κ is easy to compute, and the paper already does this for C19. Without it, statements such as "omissions dominate on every set" or "the transcript cuts commission credit faster" have no stated precision; the latter is in fact *not* significant (J12, McNemar p = 0.11) and reverses on the main study (3 → 5 of 6). *Fix:* add item-bootstrap intervals for every κ in Figure 1 and Table 1, and soften the Table 1 caption.

4. **The headline pre-registered judge test includes unreconciled runs.** J1/J2 (κ 0.002; 53/68 failures credited) use 214 runs, 18 of which are the "harder variant", whose "counts are not reconciled and on which no claim rests" (§3, Appendix L). If the outcome records for those runs are uncertain, the executed labels behind J1/J2 are uncertain too. *Fix:* report J1/J2, C17 and the 41/44 omission statistic on the 196 reconciled runs, or state precisely what is unreconciled and why the grades of those 18 runs can be trusted.

5. **The positive control misses its registered band, and the key level is close to string matching.** The registered prediction for L3 was κ in [0.60, 0.90); it came out at 0.92-1.00. Reporting this as "above its predicted band" is honest, but it means the registered prediction failed, and the result shows the key level is much easier than anticipated. With a one-line plain description per probe, the L3 task reduces to checking whether the agent's regex matches a described property. The large L2→L3 jump shows that the judge can parse answers; it does not show that ground truth is the *only* missing ingredient (see weakness 1). The phrase "A positive control, not string matching" asserts more than the design can show. *Fix:* call L3 a parsing / positive control and move the weight of the argument onto a comparison with a keyless mechanical verifier.

6. **The judge findings may not generalise beyond an adversarial item design.** The hard probes are built so that the SAE view, which the judge also reads at L2, points to the wrong answer. A judge failing there may partly be fooled by the same trap rather than failing at verification in general. Hiding the SAE view lowers κ slightly, which argues against strong contamination, but the effect is small and untested statistically. Only one judge model (Terra) runs the full ladder; Haiku is at L2 only, and the three-model grid in C7 appears to be report-level. "Neither the prompt nor the judge model fixes this" generalises from 3 models and 2 prompts. *Fix:* state the scope explicitly (adversarial hard probes, one ladder judge), and if possible run the ladder on ordinary planted probes, where the proxy is right.

7. **Process integrity notes accumulate.** Each is minor and honestly disclosed: L1/L2 registered after Terra's results; the analysis code printing "partial" by fall-through; Holm applied by a separate script; the Luna decoy-after-refutation count coming "from the block's README, with no committed script"; judge budget guards raised mid-study; ledger events re-appended after a version-control race; an earlier-draft correction on rank-6 traps; and a 0.15 acceptance-margin relaxation for one held-out item. Together they weaken confidence in the confirmatory labels more than any one of them would. *Fix:* in the body, mark which claims rest on items with any deviation, and commit scripts for every number in the body.

## Minor issues

- **Table 1 vs Table 3 inconsistency.** Table 1 shows credited commissions going 9 → 7 from report to log, while Table 3 reports the registered J6 as "7 → 7 of 17" (L0r re-sent report → L1). A reader of the body alone sees two different baselines. Say in Table 3 that the baseline is the re-sent report.
- **Figure 1 dotted line.** It plots an *all-probe* test-retest κ (0.44) on an axis of *hard-slot* κ. The hard-slot value (0.39) is the right comparator. The appendix also describes 0.44 inconsistently: as Fleiss κ over 214 runs (C17) and as "set A, L0 vs L0r Cohen κ 0.44" (Appendix L), while C17 gives the L0-vs-replicate Cohen κ as 0.47.
- **Figure 2b** puts agent markers from the three held-out traps next to script curves computed on the shipped traps. Different item sets on the same axis invite a direct comparison the caption warns against; consider a separate panel or inset.
- The abstract's "With the key, agreement reaches 0.92-1.00" leaves out the harder variant (0.77, below its rule of 0.87). It is fine to exclude it, but say "on the three main sets" in the abstract.
- Bonferroni over "45 registered entries" is described as a lower bound on the number of tests, so the correction is mildly anti-conservative. The surviving p-values are small enough that this does not matter, but say so.
- §1's 18.7% vs 2.5% error rates depend on the designer-chosen null share (1/4), as Appendix E shows (40.8% at 0.10, 7.1% at 0.50). Move one sentence of this into the body where the number is first used.
- The abstract's "passes every item (20/21 runs)" could be misread as passing every run; "passes at least once on every item" is clearer.
- §5 has to be read in one pass with §4 to follow how "development set", "controlled block", "set A" and "Block 1" relate. A single table of populations in the body would help.

## Questions for the authors

1. What κ does a simple scripted verifier reach on the L1 log (named cue credited iff its isolating edit exceeded the 0.30 contract threshold; `none` credited iff the top-2 proxy candidates were isolated and below 0.15)?
2. Do J1/J2 and the 41/44 omission statistic hold without the 18 harder-variant runs?
3. What are item-bootstrap (6 items) intervals for κ at L2 and L3 on each set?
4. Was the judge told anything about base rates (e.g., that every episode has one null)? If a judge is told that the hard probe always has a cue, does the omission leniency disappear?
5. Was the C7 three-model grid run at report level only? If so, does any judge other than Terra reach L3?

## Single change that would raise my score by one point

Add a pre-specified *mechanical keyless verifier* on the exact-query log (and transcript) as a comparator, with item-bootstrap intervals on κ. If it also fails, the "no substitute for ground truth" claim is earned. If it succeeds, reframing the paper as "LLM judges fail to use evidence that is present" would be just as valuable and better supported.

## Overstated claims

- Title / §4: *"The judge fails for lack of ground truth, which the agent's materials do not supply."* This is not tested; no keyless non-LLM verifier is evaluated (weakness 1).
- §4 lead: *"Without ground truth, the judge never beats a trivial rule, however much of the agent's material it reads"*. "However much" is bounded by the full transcript, and the rule relies on a design base rate the judge is not given.
- §4 Robustness: *"Neither the prompt nor the judge model fixes this."* This generalises from three models and two prompts, mostly at report level.
- Table 1 caption: *"more evidence removes credited commissions faster than credited omissions"*. The registered test (J12) was not confirmed (p = 0.11), the pattern reverses on the main study (3 → 5 of 6), and on Luna's runs both fall at similar rates.
- §4 heading: *"A positive control, not string matching"*. The key level supplies a plain-language description of the answer, so it is in large part matching.
