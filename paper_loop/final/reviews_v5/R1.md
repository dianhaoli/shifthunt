# Review R1: "Searching Past the Proxy: ShiftHunt, an Environment for Certified Evaluation of Research Agents and Their Judges"

**Reviewer expertise:** statistical methodology for evaluating ML systems (measurement, reliability and validity, small-sample inference).

**Overall: 6 / 10 (borderline accept).** **Confidence: 4 / 5.**

## Summary

The paper builds a controlled interpretability-auditing environment. Agents must find which hidden text property a linear probe on Gemma-2-2B relies on, name it as a regex and remove it. They have free but sometimes deliberately misleading SAE tools (the proxy) and a small budget of exact probe queries, and a closed-form grader on hidden data scores every answer. The authors use the environment for three pre-registered studies:

1. With the SAE tools, one agent (Terra) reaches near-ceiling success at 4 to 8 exact queries, while without them it needs about 64.
2. A report-reading LLM judge is moderately self-consistent but has essentially zero agreement with the executed grade. It is systematically lenient toward "nothing found" answers and reorders a small leaderboard.
3. On "error-term traps", where the SAE ranks a decoy first, success depends on whether the agent's first batch of exact queries also tests the runner-up, and this differs between two models in the same harness.

## Strengths

1. **Gold labels that are executed, not judged, and are checked against attack.** The closed-form grader regrades 100/100 stored grades, six scripted cheaters score 0, and positive and naive controls behave as expected (Table C1). This is the kind of instrument the call asks for.
2. **A clean reliability-versus-validity demonstration for an LLM judge.** The judge's test-retest Fleiss κ is 0.39, against κ 0.002 with the certified grade (and φ ≈ 0.002, which I recomputed from Table H0). It credits 31/39 unsupported `none` answers when shown no report at all, and giving it the exact-query log does not help. The C12 control in particular pins the leniency on the judge's prior rather than on agents' prose.
3. **Pre-registration with full disclosure.** All 32 registered hypotheses are listed, including the unconfirmed ones (L3, J5′, J6, J7′, the harder variant), a cancelled arm (R6, with an explicit correction of an earlier draft), and a band table "with a hole" (C14 H2). Labels separate pre-registered, descriptive, exploratory, post hoc and reader-coded material.
4. **Curves, not a single exchange rate.** Box 2 and Appendix K show that the "blind budget equivalent" of the proxy ranges from 11 to about 50 queries depending on the reference. Reporting S_A(B) for both arms, with scripted rank-only references, is a sound recommendation for the field.
5. **The internal arithmetic is consistent.** I checked the hard-slot confusion counts (146 passes, 68 failures, agreement 0.60, κ 0.002), the per-item and per-arm totals in Tables E1 and E2, the trap gap of 0.86, and the main-study counts of 22/32 trap and 23/32 two-part passes. All agree.

## Major weaknesses

**1. Inference is effectively on 3 to 6 items, but the headline p-values are run-level, and the item-level standard is applied inconsistently.**
- Claim 1 rests on 6 items, where the item-level sign-test floor of 0.031 is reached.
- Claim 3 rests on 3 shipped traps plus 2 fresh ones, yet carries p = 2e-7 and 3e-6.
- The BB-16 contrast is called "inconclusive" because its item-level p is 0.125, even though its run-level p is 0.023. Under that same standard, L1 and L2 (3 items, item-level floor 0.125 one-sided) would also be inconclusive.
- The Bonferroni argument ("survive correction over 32 entries") is made with the run-level p-values.
- An item-clustered bootstrap over 6 clusters, as in the C1 CI of [+0.42, +0.96], is known to under-cover.

*Why it matters:* a reader takes "p = 2e-7" as strong evidence about traps in general, but it is evidence about these three traps, all from one builder.

*Fix:* make item-level analysis primary for every claim. Use exact item-level permutation or sign tests, or a mixed-effects logistic model with item random effects, and report the cluster count beside every p-value. State in one sentence that run-level p-values describe these items only.

**2. Claim 3's confirmatory status is weaker than the abstract implies.**
- L1 and L2 were frozen after Terra's Block 1 results were known (Table 2, footnote 1).
- The held-out replication's registered primary comparison (C14 H2, iso(main)) landed "outside every band".
- The contrast in the abstract, Terra 13/14 vs Luna 2/10 NAME on unseen traps, is not a registered between-model test; H1 concerns Terra alone.
- Luna's per-item split on the fresh traps is not reported.
- The fresh traps share the builder with the shipped ones, and Appendix T discloses builder-level tells (an atypical sample-logit midpoint, the runner-up reading close to the favourite). So "unseen" means unseen by agents, not unseen by builder.

*Fix:* in the abstract and in §5.3, label the Terra-vs-Luna fresh-trap result as descriptive. Report Luna's per-item counts. Either rerun with traps from an independent builder or narrow the claim to "these traps". A forced-allocation arm (§7), which would make widening a manipulation rather than an observation, would do the most to strengthen the claim.

**3. Claim 1 is one agent, and the proxy's advantage at B = 8 is matched by a trivial script.**
- At B = 8, "test the SAE's top two" scores 0.96 and Terra 23/24 (Table K1). The pre-registered B = 8 effect is therefore what a rank-only policy also gets.
- The genuinely interesting result is B = 4 (12/12 against at most 0.50 for any scripted policy), and it is explicitly unexplained.
- That B = 4 arm ran 2 to 7 days after the BB-8 arm it is compared with, and it is compared with BB-8 rather than a BB-4 arm.
- The proxy did not help Luna overall (L3, not confirmed) or Haiku on traps (0/6 vs 2/6).

*Why it matters:* the practitioner takeaway, "proxies shift the budget curve", currently holds for one model.

*Fix:* report the B = 8 result as matching the rank-only reference rather than exceeding it. Add a concurrent BB-4 arm. Run an ablation that separates ranking from free approximate scoring, for example by giving the scripted policies access to the free ablation preview. Without these, state Claim 1's scope in the title-level framing.

**4. The judge conclusion is over-generalized in places and partly a property of the design.**
- Every hard probe has a cue, so `none` on the hard slot is always wrong. The hard-slot κ therefore mostly measures the judge's leniency toward `none` (41/44 credited). The "credit any named answer" rule reaching κ 0.67 depends on this design, as §5.2 concedes.
- The sentence "a report-reading judge measures answer type, not correctness" does not follow from the evidence. The judge credits 78% of hard slots and does *not* track answer type; the trivial rule does. The judge is closer to near-uniform leniency.
- The judge never saw the SAE view or the answer key. The finding is therefore that a judge cannot verify a hidden causal property from a report. That is important for report-graded auditing benchmarks, but it is also close to expected.

*Fix:* add a hard-slot analysis restricted to named answers, together with the cue-agnostic all-slot κ (0.12), so that readers can separate leniency from discrimination. Rephrase "measures answer type". State plainly that a judge given the certifying evidence is the open, decisive test.

**5. The paper exceeds the format and is hard to read.**
- The body is about 5,400 words with 4 tables, 2 figures and roughly 440 words of references, which is likely over 8 AAAI pages.
- Figure 2 has 21 legend entries, and its title, "One proxy, many exchange rates", works against Box 2's "curves, not an exchange rate".
- Readers must hold about 15 coined terms (NAME, Open-B and BB-B, iso(main), widen, isolate, first batch, rung L0/L0r/L1/L1r, and others) and about 30 hypothesis IDs in mind.

*Fix:* move §3's reference-policy details, the C21 and PPI material, and the routes paragraph to the appendix. Cut Figure 2 to the agent points plus 2 or 3 reference curves. Drop hypothesis IDs from the body prose, keeping only Table 2. Doing this would also free space to address points 1 to 3.

## Minor issues

- **"Remove it" in the abstract overstates the task.** All 176 correctly named cues passed removal, and on traps τ is 0.15 to 0.22. At the shipped setting this is a naming task (§2.4 says so). The abstract should say so too.
- **Figure 1 is inconsistent between its source and its caption.** It is supplied as `fig2_trap_routes.svg`, and its embedded caption says "All texts are sent before any result returns". The body caption says batching "varies by agent", and blind Terra rarely sends one burst (Table M1).
- **The harder-variant n differs across the paper.** Table D1 gives Terra 7/8 vs 6/8, Table R1 gives 15 + 15 with 11/15 vs 11/15, and Appendix H counts 18 graded harder-variant runs. Please reconcile.
- **The √0.39 bound needs a caveat.** It is a correlation-based attenuation result applied to κ, and repeated sampling of one judge is not inter-rater reliability. "Heuristic" is fair, but the bound should not be read as a ceiling on κ.
- **κ intervals are not item-clustered** (acknowledged in §7). Please give intervals for the headline κ values, at least at run level.
- **The PPI statement ("worth 0.0 certified grades") is unsurprising** given r = 0.002 and could be cut.
- **Cost per pass excludes exact-query and SAE compute.** The break-even price analysis is useful, but "Open-8 $0.71" alone could mislead.
- **Self-grading.** Terra judges Terra. No self-preference was found (18/26 vs 35/42, p = 0.23), but a strong non-GPT judge is missing. Haiku as judge is a weaker model, not a peer.
- **Results kept only in released files.** Several numbers are said to be in "analysis files" or "released analysis output" (Luna's fresh-trap split, the C19 per-system rates, the other C21 orders). The ones that support claims belong in the appendix.

## Questions for the authors

1. What are the item-level tests (or a mixed-model estimate with item random effects) for L1, L2 and the C14 Terra–Luna contrast? What is Luna's per-item split on the two fresh traps?
2. At B = 4, does a scripted policy that may use the free ablation preview and own-text latent scoring approach Terra's 12/12? If so, the proxy's value is mostly free approximate scoring rather than the ranking.
3. Restricted to slots with named answers, what is the hard-slot κ at L0 and L1? Does the judge discriminate at all once `none` answers are removed?
4. Were the fresh traps checked for the midpoint and gap tells described in Appendix T, and was re-centring applied before the C14 runs?
5. Why was Open-4 compared with BB-8 rather than with a concurrent BB-4 arm?

## Single change that would raise my score by one point

Make item-level inference primary throughout: one consistent item-level test, or a mixed model with item random effects, for every confirmatory claim. Then restate Claims 1 and 3 with the resulting evidence strength, which means calling L1, L2 and the Terra–Luna fresh-trap contrast descriptive if they do not survive. The extra space this needs can be recovered by moving §3's reference-policy detail and the C21 and PPI material to the appendix.

## Overstated claims

- Abstract: *"Main tests were pre-registered."* True for Claims 1 and 2. For Claim 3, L1 and L2 were registered after Terra's results, and the abstract's fresh-trap Terra-vs-Luna NAME contrast is not a registered between-model test (H2 landed "outside every band").
- Abstract, (1): *"An agent must find the hidden cue … name it as a regex and remove it."* At the shipped τ, removal never binds for a correctly named cue (§2.4).
- §5.2: *"For this grade, a report-reading judge measures answer type, not correctness."* The trivial rule measures answer type. The judge credits 78% of hard slots and tracks neither.
- §5.3 and Table 4: *"one-sided p = 2e-7 and 3e-6"*, presented as confirming a between-model difference on traps. These are run-level tests on three trap items from one builder.
- §1: *"to our knowledge none … checks its judge against an executed grade."* This is plausible, but it should be softened to "few", since judge-versus-test-score checks exist in MLE-style settings.
