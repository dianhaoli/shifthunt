**Overall: 7/10** (accept; a solid workshop paper). **Confidence: 4/5.**

## Summary

The paper presents ShiftHunt, an interpretability-auditing environment. An agent must find the hidden cue that a linear probe on Gemma-2-2B (block-12 residual, mean-pooled) relies on, express it as a regex, and list Gemma Scope SAE latents whose mean ablation removes the cue's effect. The SAE tools are a free but fallible proxy. "Trap" probes are weight-edited so that the SAE view ranks a decoy above the true cue. Exact probe queries are rationed, and a closed-form grader runs every answer on hidden factorial data. Three pre-registered claims follow:

1. With the SAE tools, one model (Terra) beats matched no-proxy arms at 4 and 8 exact queries.
2. A report-reading LLM judge is reliable-ish (κ 0.39 self-agreement) but invalid (κ 0.002 against the grade). The exact-query log does not fix it, and the judge reorders a small leaderboard.
3. On traps, outcomes depend on whether the agent's first query batch also tests the proxy's runner-up. Terra does this and the smaller Luna rarely does.

All traps put the true cue at proxy rank 2, so the paper tests one-step widening, not deep search.

## Strengths

1. **Ground truth stronger than a judge, plus red-teaming.** The grade is computed in closed form. Regrading reproduces 100/100 stored runs, six scripted cheaters all score 0, and a positive and a naive control behave as expected (Table C1). This is exactly the "how good is the instrument" question the call asks.
2. **Reporting discipline is unusually good.** Every registered hypothesis appears in Appendix R, including failures (L3, J5′, J6, J7′, the harder-variant prediction) and C14 H2, reported as "outside every band" rather than quietly relabelled. Errors from earlier drafts are corrected openly (the rank-6 bank claim; the 3.9 vs 1.7 figure). I checked many numbers against the appendices and they agree: Table E1 per-item counts sum to the body's 23/24, 6/24, 16/24 and 11/12 vs 2/12; the H0/H1 confusion counts reproduce κ-level agreement 0.60 and the per-agent judge-implied rates; the Fisher p-values for L1 (2.2e-7) and X3-H1 (1.5e-5) are correct; the break-even price of $0.006 follows from Table J1.
3. **The judge study is decomposed well.** Reliability is separated from validity (test-retest κ 0.39 vs κ 0.002 against the grade). An evidence ladder, a placebo log, a report-free control showing that the `none` leniency is a prior (31/39 vs 1/39), cross-judge and cross-prompt checks, and the observation that "credit any named answer" (κ 0.67) beats every judge rung together make a convincing case that this judge measures answer type.
4. **"Curves, not an exchange rate" (Box 2) is a useful corrective.** The paper shows that the implied blind-query exchange rate ranges from 11 to about 50 depending on the reference, and reports S(B) for both arms instead.
5. **Limits are stated honestly.** Removal is only a gate at the shipped τ. All traps are at rank 2. The fresh traps are held out from the agents, not from the builder. Disclosed tells (Appendix T) are listed. The B = 4 advantage is called "not identified" rather than explained away.

## Major weaknesses

**1. A contract-band tell may explain Terra's trap selectivity and its B = 4 advantage. This threatens construct validity for Claims 1 and 3.**
By Appendix T, on shipped traps the [main] reads 0.17-0.24 topic units through the proxy and the [decoy] reads 0.33-0.40. The prompt promises that every non-main property is at most 0.15 and every main cue at least 0.30. A proxy runner-up reading inside the forbidden band (0.15, 0.30) is therefore a near-direct signal that the proxy is wrong on this probe. On ordinary probes the proxy captures about 0.65 of true effects, and true non-cue effects are at most 0.15, so non-cues there should read well below that band.

Why this matters: an agent exploiting this tell will:
- widen only on traps (Terra widens on 11/12 traps vs 4/48 ordinary probes);
- beat every rank-only reference at B = 4, since those references ignore magnitudes;
- replicate on fresh traps from the same builder.

None of these would reflect the "searching past the proxy" skill the paper wants to measure. The paper does list "closeness" as an unscored candidate trigger, and Appendix T tests the band only as a zero-query naming rule, not as a trigger for where to spend queries.

Fix:
- Report the rate at which the proxy runner-up falls in (0.15, 0.30) on ordinary, null, two-part and trap probes.
- Add a magnitude-aware reference policy that widens when that band is hit. Compare its trap/ordinary widening rates and its B = 4 score with Terra's.
- Ideally, build a tell-matched control: ordinary probes whose runner-up reads in the band, or traps whose [main] reads just below 0.15.

**2. The judge's invalidity is partly built into what the judge sees. The paper needs an evidence-ceiling baseline.**
A judge at L0 sees no data, and the failures it misses are mostly omissions (41/44 `none` failures credited). From a report alone, a correct `none` cannot be told apart from a wrong one, because the agents are not told how many probes have cues. Even at L1, an omission leaves no trace in the log. So "no judge separates `none`" is close to an identifiability statement about the evidence, not only a fact about LLM judges.

Why this matters: practitioners need to know whether the remedy is a better judge or a different evidence protocol, such as requiring the agent to exact-test each aligned candidate before claiming `none`.

Fix:
- Score a scripted judge with the L1 log: credit a named cue only if it was isolated with an effect above threshold, and credit `none` only if every aligned candidate was isolated. Report its κ as the ceiling an LLM judge could reach from the same evidence.
- Separately, test a judge given the design prior (the hard probe always has a cue). This is cheap and would show whether the leniency is a prior that can be fixed.

**3. The item-level evidence is thin. The prominent p-values are run-level and conditional on 3-6 items.**
Claim 3 rests on three shipped traps (L1/L2 p = 2e-7 and 3e-6, run-level) and two fresh ones (C14, p = 0.002 on two items). The item-level sign-test floor with three items is 0.25 two-sided, so no item-level test of Claim 3 is possible. L1/L2 were also frozen after Terra's Block 1 results were known. The judge κ intervals are not item-clustered, even though judge errors plausibly cluster by item (e.g., traps that invite `none`). The paper discloses all of this, but Table 2 and the abstract lead with the run-level numbers.

Fix:
- In Table 2, put the item-level result next to every run-level p-value, and label L1/L2 as "registered after the comparator was observed".
- Give item-clustered κ intervals for J1 and C8.
- If budget allows, add three or more fresh traps. Even one per new builder seed would help.

**4. Claim 3 ("batch width separates pass from fail; width differs by model") is mostly observational and confounded with general capability.**
- The route result (25/26 vs 5/21) is partly true by definition. Conditional on isolating the [main], it is not established within agent (p = 0.15).
- Terra supplies 17 of the 26 wide-first-batch runs, so agent and route are confounded.
- Luna is weaker across the board (two-part 4/18 vs 12/12). The capability adjustment that predicts about 7/18 widenings is post hoc and run-level.
- On fresh traps, Luna isolates the [main] in 3/10, so "rarely widens" is weaker there.

Fix:
- Restate Claim 3 as a pre-registered Terra-vs-Luna difference plus exploratory route evidence.
- Add the forced-allocation arm the paper already proposes in its Limitations: instruct or constrain the first batch to cover the top two candidates. That would test widening causally.
- Alternatively, add a second same-harness pair of models with similar general capability.

**5. Ecological validity of the SAE failure, and the removal half of the task.**
The traps are adversarial weight edits that change 36-61% of ‖w‖ and inject a decoy direction that reads through the SAE reconstruction but not through true residual flips. Real SAE failures (dark matter, feature absorption or splitting, error-term reliance) may have a different signature, and the natural rate π_t(r) is unknown. Separately, at the shipped τ, removal never binds (176/176 correctly named cues pass; τ on traps is 0.15-0.22). ShiftHunt is therefore a naming task, and the SHIFT-style erasure part is effectively unevaluated.

Why this matters: an interpretability-agent benchmark whose proxy failures are synthetic, and whose erasure test is nominal, says little about how auditing agents fare against real SAE failures.

Fix:
- Report the rank of the true cue among bank probes trained without the weight edit (the natural π_t(r)), even if it is mostly rank 1.
- Ship the f = 0.9, k = 8 setting as a "removal-binding" track with its own agent results, or drop "certified … removal" language from the framing.

**6. Length and readability.**
The body is at or over the 8-page limit by my estimate (about 5,440 words with tables, 4 tables, 2 figures, 2 boxes, 31 references). The prose relies on about 40 codes and evidence labels. The hypothesis ids C1/C2/C4 collide with "Claim 1/2/3", and almost every paragraph points into an appendix. A workshop reader cannot reconstruct the argument from the body alone.

Fix:
- Cut §5.3 routes, the cost paragraph and the C19/C21/PPI details to the appendix.
- Rename hypothesis ids (e.g., "H-proxy", "H-judge") so they do not collide with the claims.
- Keep one evidence label per result.
- Use a full-width figure* for Figure 2 with a third of its 18 legend entries.

## Minor issues

- **Two success definitions without warning.** §5.2 "Consequences" uses hard-slot pass rates, while §5.1 uses NAME. For example, the certified BB-16→BB-64 change is +0.25 in §5.2, but NAME gives 16/24 → 24/24 = +0.33; BB-64 − BB-8 is +0.67 in §5.2 vs C2 = +0.75. State the metric in each place.
- **Harder-variant numbers do not agree:**
  - Table D1: "Terra 7/8 vs 6/8 on the same items".
  - Table R1: "15 + 15, 11/15 vs 11/15".
  - Appendix H: 18 harder-variant runs judged.
- **Token-cap framing.** The body says that failing every run above any cap ≥ 86k "keeps the gain ≥ +0.71". The appendix shows +0.46 at the Open-8 median cap. Report that number in the body too.
- **Figure 2:**
  - The title says "(measured)", but the figure includes [priv] abstract-model curves.
  - The star marker means both "Terra B = 4" (panel a) and "fresh held-out traps" (panel b).
  - In panel (b), agent points that belong at rank 2 are drawn near rank 1.5, so they look like they sit between ranks.
- **Figure file naming.** Figure 1's file is named `fig2_trap_routes.svg`, and the SVG figures were not in the package.
- **Ambiguous wording.** "Terra's credit for correct `none`: 247/263 → 6/263" means Terra *as judge*. Say so.
- **Bonferroni over rows.** The correction is applied over the 32 rows of Table R1, but some rows bundle several hypotheses (J3/J4, R1/R4). Count hypotheses instead, or say why rows are the right unit.
- **Unsupported attenuation bound.** "Caps its agreement with the grade near √0.39 ≈ 0.62" applies a Spearman-style correction to κ on binary labels. It is called heuristic, but it adds little. Drop it or justify it.
- **Proprietary harnesses unpinned.** Give the model snapshot identifiers and the Codex CLI / Claude Code versions.
- **"Unseen traps" in the abstract.** These are unseen by agents but built by the same builder. Qualify the phrase once in the abstract.
- **Proxy value is negative for Haiku on traps.** Haiku's trap result (0/6 with the proxy vs 2/6 without) and Luna's non-confirmed L3 suggest the proxy's value depends strongly on the model and can be negative. That deserves one sentence in the body, since misuse is a term of Box 2.

## Questions for the authors

1. On ordinary and null probes, how often does the proxy runner-up read in (0.15, 0.30) topic units? Does a magnitude-aware policy that widens on that signal reproduce Terra's 12/12 at B = 4 and its 11/12 vs 4/48 selectivity?
2. What κ does a scripted, evidence-checking judge reach on the L1 logs? Does telling the LLM judge that the hard probe always has a cue remove the `none` leniency?
3. Without the weight edit, how often is the true cue at proxy rank 1 among bank probes? Have you seen any naturally occurring rank-2+ cases?
4. Were the L1/L2 thresholds chosen with Terra's 11/12 in view, and would any plausible Luna outcome have failed them?
5. Which harder-variant numbers are correct (7/8 vs 6/8, or 11/15 vs 11/15), and on which population?
6. Do agents ever estimate the SAE error-term contribution directly, by comparing a `probe_query` logit with the latent-gradient reconstruction on the same text? If they did, would the trap be trivial?

## Single change that would raise my score by one point

Rule out the contract-band tell (Major weakness 1). Report the in-band rate of the proxy runner-up by probe type, and add a magnitude-aware reference policy, plus ideally a tell-matched control. If Terra's selectivity and its B = 4 advantage survive, Claims 1 and 3 become much stronger. If they do not, the paper gains a sharp, publishable lesson about proxy tells in agent benchmarks.

## Overstated claims

- Abstract: **"A report-reading judge is invalid."** This is true for this grade and this configuration. But for the omission errors that dominate, no judge given only the report could be valid, so the sentence reads more general than the evidence supports. See Major weakness 2.
- Contributions, Claim 3: **"on traps, the width of the first batch on the trap probe separates pass from fail."** This rests on exploratory development-set routes. It is partly true by definition, and the conditional within-agent test is p = 0.15.
- Abstract: **"on unseen traps Terra names the cue in 13/14 runs"**. The traps are held out from agents but come from the same builder, and the disclosed tells may carry over (Appendix T).
- Title: **"Certified Evaluation of Research Agents"**. The certification is naming against the generator, removal is a nominal gate at the shipped τ, and the evidence covers one interpretability environment.
- Abstract: **"it reorders a nine-system leaderboard"**. This is exploratory: 2 of 13 reversals are robust and no test is given. That is acceptable, but it should carry the [expl] label wherever it appears.
