# Comparison H3: first (paper_M) vs second (paper_N)

Verdict: first is stronger (clear margin). Scores: first 6.8, second 6.0.

## First (M)
- One thesis, stated in the title and abstract: an LLM judge without the answer key cannot grade research agents. This fits the CFP directly (LLM-as-judge validity, ground truth stronger than opinion, reliability vs validity).
- Evidence ladder L0-L3 across three run sets, with a pre-registered positive control (key level kappa 0.92-1.00). This answers the obvious objection that the judge is simply broken. It is the main substantive gain over N, whose judge claim is "untested with key".
- Honest statistics: Holm miss on the transcript gain, the trivial-rule baseline, and the harder variant explicitly carrying no claim.
- Clearer structure: results section 4 (judge) and section 5 (agents), single pre-registration table, shorter body (~5.4k words vs ~5.9k).
- Section 5.4 (contract signal) is a thoughtful confound analysis, though dense.
## Second (N)
- Frames ShiftHunt as an environment plus a proxy-and-gold recipe (Box 2 on curves vs exchange rate). Broader contribution, better fits a resource/benchmark reading and gives the recipe.
- Includes leaderboard reordering (nine systems), prediction-powered inference worth-0 argument, cost per pass, and a figure of trap routes.
- Three-claim structure is more diffuse; judge claim is weaker (no key, no transcript level), and it leans on a trivial rule beating every rung without resolving why.
- Heavier jargon and label load (C6 J5', X3-H1, etc. in body tables) and a longer body; more likely to overrun 8 pages.
## Both
- Appendices carry large material (non-reviewable at 6-8 pp); both are very dense and abbreviation-heavy; both single domain and 3-6 items; Terra judging Terra self-grading concern in both.
- Matched compute caveat addressed in both (tokens not matched, justified).
