# Comparison H2: paper_M ("first") vs paper_N ("second")

Venue: AAAI-27 workshop "Effective and Efficient Evaluation for AI Scientists", full paper, 6-8 pp.
Inputs read: paper_M.md, paper_N.md, cfp.md, and the figures each paper cites (fig1_ladder.png and fig2_budget.png for M; fig2_trap_routes.svg and fig2_measured.png for N).

## Verdict
**First (M) is the stronger submission, by a clear margin.** First: 6.5. Second: 5.5.

## What each version is
- **First (M): "No Substitute for Ground Truth".** The paper is built around one evaluation result. A judge-evidence ladder (L0 report, L1 adding the exact-query log, L2 adding the full transcript, L3 adding the answer key) is scored on three fixed run sets. Without the key, κ is at most 0.26 everywhere, which is below a trivial "credit any named answer" rule. With the key, κ is 0.92-1.00. Wrong "nothing found" answers (omissions) make up most of the false credit. Agent results (budget curves, Terra vs Luna widening, three held-out traps with 20/21 vs 2/17) come second and are labelled descriptive. A new §5.4 discloses that the contract leaks a "gap" signal that could partly explain widening.
- **Second (N): "Searching Past the Proxy".** This version is framed as an environment/benchmark paper with three claims: (1) the proxy shifts Terra's budget curve, (2) the report-reading judge is invalid (L0/L1 only; the transcript and answer-key levels are explicitly "untested"), and (3) the width of the first batch on the trap separates agents (two fresh traps, 13/14 vs 2/10). It carries more environment detail (a probe-type table, generator, grader), a budgeted-search decomposition (Box 2) with a "recipe", leaderboard reordering (τ_b 0.23, 13/36 pairs flip), and a PPI "worth 0 grades" result.

## Where the first (M) is better
1. **Substance on the judge question.** M adds the full-transcript level (L2) and the answer-key positive control (L3). N names exactly these as untested gaps. Because of them, "the judge fails" becomes "the judge fails for lack of ground truth": a reader can see that the judge can grade, and that the agent's materials cannot stand in for the key. This answers the obvious reviewer objection to N.
2. **Fit to the call.** The title and thesis line up with "ground truth stronger than reviewer opinion", "LLM-as-a-judge reliability" and "process-level evaluation from agent execution traces". L2 is a trace-level judge, so M speaks to that last topic directly.
3. **Focus and clarity.** M has one headline, a running example, and an "Is this obvious?" paragraph that heads off the triviality objection. It uses far fewer internal codes. N's body leans on ledger codes (C14 H2, J5′, X4, L0r, BB-16, [priv], [reader]) and a long "Terms" paragraph, which makes it hard to read.
4. **Figures.** M's Figure 1 (the ladder) carries the main finding at a glance. Its Figure 2 is a clean two-panel plot. N's Figure 2 has about 20 legend entries and is unreadable at AAAI column width.
5. **Length compliance.** M's body is about 5.4k words including references, with 2 figures, 3 tables and 1 box. N's is about 5.9k words with 2 figures (one needs full width), 4 tables and 2 boxes. M is much more likely to fit 8 AAAI pages. N very likely overflows.
6. **Honesty about confounds.** M's §5.4 (the gap signal in the contract) and its explicit statement that agents show only one-step widening make the agent claims appropriately modest. M also adds a third held-out trap and more held-out runs (21/17 vs 14/10).
7. **Takeaway.** M gives practitioners a usable lesson: grade by execution where possible, and audit "nothing found" verdicts first.

## Where the second (N) is better
1. **Environment and benchmark content.** N has a probe-type table, the generator, the knobs, and a full grader description (removal threshold τ, the closed-form ablation, and the fact that removal is nominal on traps). It would be easier to reproduce and reuse.
2. **"How good is the instrument" breadth.** N has the leaderboard-reordering result (τ_b 0.23, 13/36 pairs flip), the PPI analysis (verdicts worth 0 certified grades), the test-retest attenuation bound, and judged-curve distortions. These hit the call's reliability-vs-validity and ranking-sensitivity topics, and M drops them from the body.
3. **Conceptual framing.** N has the budgeted-search decomposition (Box 2: information value, blind shortfall, misuse), a portable recipe mapped onto MLE-bench, and cost per pass under matched budget (Open-8 $0.71 vs BB-8 $3.86).
4. **Statistical detail in the body.** N reports item-clustered bootstrap CIs, sign-test floors, and a capability-adjusted widening expectation (about 7 expected vs 0 observed for Luna).

## Concerns with the first (M) that keep it near 6.5
- The L3 positive control is close to expected: a judge holding the key is largely matching a regex against a description. The real result is the L2 failure. M argues this, but reviewers may still discount the headline.
- Figure 1 includes a "harder variant" whose counts are "not reconciled". Putting unreconciled data in the main figure, even with a disclaimer, looks unfinished.
- There is a small internal inconsistency on the commission-credit baseline. The body and Table 1 say 9 → 3 of 17; Table 3 says 7 → 7 and 7 → 3. This is probably L0 vs the L0r re-send, but the body does not explain it.
- Terra judges its own model family, so self-grading bias is only partly addressed. Every hard probe has a cue, which inflates the trivial rule.
- Six items, all trap mains at proxy rank 2, and a single construction script. Agent claims rest on 3-6 items.

## Concerns with the second (N)
- Its central judge claim is limited to L0/L1, and the paper itself concedes the transcript and answer-key levels are untested. This is the main hole a reviewer would point to.
- It is too dense for a workshop body: three claims plus an environment plus a recipe plus a 12-row ledger. It probably exceeds 8 pages.
- Figure 2 is overloaded.
- C14 rests on only two items.
