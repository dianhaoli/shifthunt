**The nested cue is thinkable but listed late.** A text-only listing experiment (Exp F; 420 calls; added after an interim look) separates hypothesis formation from tool use. Each prompt shows a 128-note sample with eight label-aligned properties, and the model lists up to 20 candidate regexes. On the shipped two-part samples, the nested cue is among the 20 in 42/60 answers but **among the first eight, the size of the exact budget, in only 21/60**, vs 140/240 for plain cues (p = 0.0014, descriptive) and 30/60 for trap mains. On generated cues (Table 5), listing drops sharply for properties of a property and conjunctions under either scoring rule; the decline for typographic and positional cues is an artefact of case-sensitive matching. The pattern parallels the under-hypothesized conjunctive causes of GX-Chen et al. (2025), here as an *omission before any test*.
